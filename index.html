Stuck swap? Who moves next?

Paste one ID. See what has really happened to your money, and who has to act next.

Paste the transaction ID from your wallet's history (it may be called a hash or Tx ID), or a link to it on a site like Etherscan or NearBlocks. The deposit address your swap app showed you works too, and so does an Omni bridge ID like Near:563760.

Where do I find this?

Open your wallet's activity, tap the swap, and copy its transaction ID. If your swap app gave you an address to send coins to, you can paste that address instead.

      How Intent Sentinel works

      A cross-chain swap (your coins start on one blockchain and arrive on another) passes through several services. When it stalls, it's hard to tell who has to act. Intent Sentinel reads the public records and tells you.

      Four steps

      1. You paste one IDThe transaction ID from your wallet, a block explorer link, an Omni bridge ID or your deposit address.
      2. It reads public recordsYour browser asks the blockchains and bridges directly. Cryppit runs no server in between.
      3. It labels every factEach step is marked proven, reported, inferred or not visible (what these mean).
      4. Fixed rules decideA fixed list of written rules names the next move. If proof is missing, it says so instead of guessing.

      How to read the picture

      1. TickThe blockchain shows this step happened
      3. GlowThis is as far as the records go
      5. DashedNot proven yet, or not reached

      A beam then points from the last stop to whoever can act next. If nobody needs to act, it points to "Nobody". Each stop also carries a label. A step we couldn't check says Unknown, and a stop the money hasn't reached says Not reached.

      Three real cases

      0.02 ETH, NEAR → EthereumThe transfer started on NEAR but never finished. Only the bridge's operator (an Omni relayer) can run the next step, so the sender can't finish it alone.
      Solana → NEAR transferThe deposit on Solana is proven, but the Omni bridge's own records don't show it. So the bridge step is marked Inferred, not Proven.
      5 BTC refundedThe bitcoin was sent in, received by NEAR Intents, then sent back. All three steps are proven, and the amounts match exactly. Nobody needs to act.

      Who it can name

      NEAR IntentsThe NEAR service that holds your coins between the steps of a swap.

      Why you can trust the answer

      Same evidence, same answerEach check gets a case number and a fingerprint: a short code made from the answer and the blockchain records behind it. Anyone can run it again and compare. If something new happens on the blockchain, the code changes.
      A file you can hand overDownload the case as a file for a support ticket. It holds the blockchain records and the answer, with its labels. If someone edits part of it, the fingerprint no longer matches. Someone could still forge a whole new file, so the real test is to run the check again, or compare it with the daily record on NEAR.
      A daily record on NEAREach day's case files can be combined into one code and saved on the NEAR blockchain, so nobody can change them later without it showing. The three example cases were saved on NEAR's test network on 6 Nov (account sentinel.cryppit.testnet).

      What it can't tell you

      • It names who acts next, not who's to blame.
      • It can't move your money or make anyone act.
      • If a public record doesn't answer, you get no answer rather than a guess.
      • An example case shows the records as they were on the day we saved it.

      It never asks for your recovery phrase or private key. If anyone messages you offering to recover your money, it's a scam.

      Your privacy

      What you paste goes straight from your browser to public services: blockchain servers, bridge records and block explorers. The page also downloads 1Click's public list of coins; that request doesn't include what you pasted. If you paste a deposit address, it goes to 1Click (the NEAR Intents swap service), because that's how we find the swap. The page has no tracking, saves nothing in your browser and doesn't tell other sites where you came from.

      Words used here

      Blockchain (network)A public record of transactions, like Bitcoin, Ethereum, Solana or NEAR. Anyone can read it.
      Transaction ID (hash)The unique code of one transaction. Your wallet shows it in the swap's details.
      Block explorerA website that shows what's on a blockchain, like Etherscan or NearBlocks.
      Deposit addressA one-time address your swap app gives you to send coins to.
      Cross-chain swapYour coins start on one blockchain and arrive on another.
      BridgeA service that moves coins from one blockchain to another.
      Omni bridgeNEAR's bridge between NEAR and other blockchains.
      RelayerThe operator that runs a bridge step. Some steps only it can run.
      NEAR IntentsThe NEAR service that holds your coins between the steps of a swap.
      1ClickThe NEAR Intents swap service many wallets use.
      IntentA swap request ("swap this for that"). Companies called solvers compete to fill it.
      PayoutThe coins sent to you on the receiving blockchain.
      Refund addressWhere your coins go back to if the swap can't happen.
      Smart contractA program that lives on a blockchain. Some wallets are smart contracts too.
      Private stepSince 27 Oct 2026, the middle of a 1Click swap happens privately on NEAR, so nobody outside can see it.
      FingerprintA short code made from the answer and its records. It changes if anything changes.
      Case fileThe answer, the proof behind it and the file you can download.
      Test networkA practice copy of a blockchain, where coins have no value.
